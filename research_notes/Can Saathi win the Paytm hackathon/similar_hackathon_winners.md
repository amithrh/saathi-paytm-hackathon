# Winners and finalists of comparable Indian fintech / Indic-voice AI hackathons, 2025–2026

Scope: India, 2025–2026, fintech-company-run or Indic-language AI hackathons comparable to the Paytm Build for India AI Hackathon. Each entry notes organiser and date. "Confirmed" = stated in a cited source; "Inference" = my reading of the evidence. Research date: 31 Oct 2026 (the day of the Paytm Bengaluru devSemble finals).

Overall caveat: official per-city winner lists for the Paytm hackathon itself, and formal judging rubrics for almost every fintech-company-run event, are NOT published. Most "why judges picked them" evidence is from organiser recaps and one or two judge quotes, not scoring sheets. Treat the pattern section as inference built on thin but consistent evidence.

---

## Key question 1: Sarvam AI hackathons and Indic voice-agent challenges

### Takeaway
Across three Sarvam-partnered events (Great Bengaluru Hackathon Mar 2025, Warpspeed Jun 2025, BuildIn' Hours Aug 2026), winners were working prototypes solving one narrow, India-specific problem (MSME tender discovery, a multilingual loan advisor, a merchant ops agent). Voice/Indic was the enabling stack, not the pitch; the only published judge quote credits "real problem + working prototype + presentation."

### Cited Findings
- Great Bengaluru Hackathon (Sarvam x HackCulture, 15–16 Mar 2025, Bengaluru): 4,700 registrations, 150 teams, 50 projects built on the Sarvam track — [Sarvam on X](https://x.com/SarvamAI/status/1905165495042535889)
- The Sarvam Track (Track 2) problem statement was "a Multilingual Conversational Loan Advisor to enhance accessibility in financial decision-making"; HackerEarth was the evaluation partner, 9 jurors, 25+ mentors — [PES University](https://news.pes.edu/11806/); [HackCulture recap](https://hackculture.io/blog/great-bengaluru-hackathon-2025-success-story)
- Standout Sarvam-track project: LoanSaathi (IIT Guwahati) — multilingual conversational loan advisor with dialect-specific tuning, real-time document parsing, fraud detection and financial-literacy support, delivered via WhatsApp integration. The recap labels it "Product shipped" but does not say whether it supported voice or name the languages — [HackCulture recap](https://hackculture.io/blog/great-bengaluru-hackathon-2025-success-story)
- Warpspeed Agentic AI Hackathon (Lightspeed with Sarvam, magicpin, AWS, Google Cloud, Bhindi, Base; 21–22 Jun 2025, Bengaluru): 2,000+ participants, 150+ shortlisted, 64 projects showcased, $12,200 prize pool — [Devfolio recap](https://devfolio.co/blog/warpspeed-2025-recap/); [Devfolio event page](https://warpspeed2025.devfolio.co/)
- Warpspeed grand prize ($2,500): DreamOps, an on-call debugging agent (dev-tools, not fintech); 1st runner-up PragyanAI, a multilingual learning agent; 2nd runner-up PlushieOS, an emotional AI companion in a soft toy — [Lightspeed India on X](https://x.com/LightspeedIndia/status/1937084673496404199)
- Warpspeed magicpin-track winner ($500): Plattr, "an AI-native, multi-agent assistant designed to streamline operations for local merchants operating across platforms like Swiggy, Zomato, Magicpin, and ONDC", using "language-agnostic voice actions powered by Sarvam AI" (2-person team) — [Akshat Gupta, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/agupta28/)
- Sarvam described Warpspeed as teams using the Sarvam Stack "to build voice-led and multilingual AI Agents. Each idea was rooted in a real problem, focused on building something useful that could hold up" — [Sarvam on X](https://x.com/SarvamAI/status/1937385814956159108)
- Sarvam BuildIn' Hours (Sarvam x HackCulture x Elevation Capital Basecamp, 9 Aug 2026, Zo House Bengaluru, 12-hour in-person): 6,272 applied in 48 hours, 100 selected; tracks Voice AI, Document Intelligence, Multilingual AI, AI Agents; ₹5 lakh pool across top 3; teams had "one chance to demo to an expert jury at the end of the night" — [Zo House Substack](https://blrxzo.substack.com/p/1-in-62-made-it-in-sarvam-ais-hackathon); [SMEStreet](https://smestreet.in/infocus/zo-house-bengaluru-hosts-sarvam-ais-buildin-hours-hackathon-6272-applied-in-48-hours-100-got-in-12391041)
- BuildIn' Hours winners: (1) Government Tender Discovery and Application for MSMEs, on Sarvam Vision, "praised for solving a real problem with a working prototype"; (2) Storytelling for Kids in Indic Languages; (3) Setu, visual communication tool for people who cannot speak, "one of the best-presented projects" — [Zo House Substack](https://blrxzo.substack.com/p/1-in-62-made-it-in-sarvam-ais-hackathon)
- Judge Arsh Goyal: "The entire space of Voice AI has matured a lot from what we saw in 2024" and that the ideas addressed real problems in India — [Zo House Substack](https://blrxzo.substack.com/p/1-in-62-made-it-in-sarvam-ais-hackathon)
- A Sarvam-hackathon project "CollectAI" (loan-collections voice-call simulator, Hinglish, Saaras v3 STT + sarvam-105b + Bulbul v3 TTS with barge-in) exists on GitHub; no placing stated — [GitHub dakshitapokharna/collect](https://github.com/dakshitapokharna/collect)

### Inferences
- Across Sarvam events, the single most consistent winner trait is an MSME/merchant or "underserved user" problem with a visibly working build; grand prizes did not go to the most voice-heavy project (DreamOps was a dev-tools agent).
- Sarvam's own framing ("rooted in a real problem… could hold up") plus the one judge quote suggests robustness under live demo mattered more than feature breadth.
- Lending-adjacent projects (LoanSaathi) win when the organiser literally sets the problem statement; no evidence they win on open tracks.

### Gaps
- No published scoring rubric for any Sarvam event.
- Sarvam-track winner at Warpspeed is not named in the Devfolio recap or Lightspeed post.
- Team names/sizes and demo formats for GBH 2025 winners not published.

## Key question 4: RBI HaRBInger 2024–2026

### Takeaway
HaRBInger 2025 (launched Oct 2025, results May 2026) rewarded compliance-heavy infrastructure (tokenised KYC, offline CBDC, fraud/trust) with multi-month development phases and ₹40 lakh prizes; a public-sector bank won two awards, showing judges prize deployability inside regulated rails over consumer UX.

### Cited Findings
- HaRBInger 2025 launched 23 Oct 2025; theme "Secure Banking: Powered by Identity, Integrity and Inclusivity"; problem statements Tokenised KYC, Offline CBDC (e₹), Enhancing Trust; prizes ₹40 lakh winner / ₹20 lakh runner-up per problem statement, ₹20 lakh best all-woman team, ₹5 lakh stipend per shortlisted team for the solution-development phase; mentoring then presentation to an "eminent jury" — [RBI press release](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=61485)
- Results (May 2026): 496 teams from 15 countries; winners included startups, independent innovators and a public-sector bank, the first time a bank directly participated; results tied to RBI press release 2025–2026/1369 — [Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/05/19/rbi-harbinger-2025-winners-feature-india-cbdc-tokenised-kyc-ai-fraud-tools/) (snippet only; page returned 403 on fetch)
- Bank of Baroda: Best All-Women Team award for "NIDAAN" (recognised for "strong design, inclusivity focus, and practical application in secure digital banking") and runner-up in Offline CBDC (Baroda Research and Innovation Centre, secure transactions in low-connectivity environments) — [PSU Connect, 12 May 2026](https://www.psuconnect.in/bank-news/bank-of-baroda-wins-big-at-rbi-harbinger-2025-hackathon-with-two-major-awards)

### Inferences
- HaRBInger is structurally unlike a one-day Paytm sprint (months-long, stipend-funded prototype phase), so its lessons transfer mainly on compliance framing: solutions that embed regulatory constraints in design are what a regulator-run jury rewards.

### Gaps
- Full winner list for Tokenised KYC, Offline CBDC winner, and Enhancing Trust not retrieved (Crypto Times and RBI results page not fetchable). No published scoring criteria.

- Top 100 pitched to a 30-member jury; organisers focused on "practical, real-world impact"; judges observed a trend toward "domain-specific, production-ready tools that can plug into enterprise pipelines, provide auditability, and scale"; winning projects reflected a move "away from prototype-style demos" — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/artificial-intelligence/google-cloud-gen-ai-hackathon-2025-winners-use-cases-and-what-270000-developers-built)

- No fintech-track winner was identified in any Google India event; no "Google Pay hackathon" found — [search results](https://yourstory.com/2025/12/google-cloud-gen-ai-exchange-hackathon-the-10-innovations-that-stood-out-this-year)

## Key question 12: Common reasons finalists lost

### Takeaway
Direct loss post-mortems for fintech events in India were not found; the closest first-person accounts (Smart India Hackathon 2025 winners/losers) consistently name the same failure modes: over-long architecture talk with no demo shown, demo connectivity failures, and misreading what the organiser actually wanted. Large-sponsor organisers now explicitly disfavour "prototype-style demos."

### Cited Findings
- "The team next to them had a 12-minute presentation, judges cut them off at 5, they were still explaining architecture when time ran out and never showed a working demo"; judge feedback cited "problem fit, working demo, and robustness rather than code quality or tech stack"; team swapped a failing ML model for rules + LLM API and judges "never asked if it was real ML" — [Reskilll blog](https://reskilll.com/blogs/how-i-won-smart-india-hackathon-lessons-36-hours-changed-everything)
- SIH 2025 winner: internet failed mid-demo, judges briefly assumed it was broken; a judge later returned unprompted, demo ran perfectly, "that moment turned the tide" — [Medium, Kartikay Singh](https://medium.com/@kartikaysingh4329/how-i-won-indias-biggest-hackathon-sih-2025-69dafd1d792e)
- Losing finalist critique: when judges can't engage with technical depth, "the competition becomes about who can be loudest about their presentation"; no scores or leaderboard published — [Medium, "The SIH Illusion"](https://medium.com/@raipratik0101/why-indias-biggest-hackathon-is-broken-the-sih-illusion-99db2b803976)
- Winning projects "further along the business continuum" beat experimental builds; author lost after presenting a working trading bot because organisers wanted startups — [Medium, Brian](https://medium.com/@brianontheweb/how-to-win-a-hackathon-for-fun-and-profit-ca7d02919629) (not India-specific)
- Gen AI Exchange 2025: winners moved "away from prototype-style demos" toward production-ready, auditable, enterprise-integrable systems — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/artificial-intelligence/google-cloud-gen-ai-hackathon-2025-winners-use-cases-and-what-270000-developers-built)

### Inferences
- "Generic chatbot" as a loss reason is inferred, not quoted: with dozens of near-identical Hindi voice merchant copilots in the Paytm field, a generic one is indistinguishable, and organisers' stated preference for auditable, action-taking agents implies chat-only entries rank lower.
- "No business model" is not cited as a loss reason by any Indian judge in scope; the business lens appears mainly via "impact/relevance" rubric lines (20–25%) rather than monetisation.

### Gaps

## Key question 14: Repeatable patterns across winners (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Winners in this cohort share: one narrow, India-specific underserved user; a build that actually runs in the room; vernacular/voice as delivery not as the pitch; an agent that takes a concrete action (file, order, claim, reminder) with visible guardrails; and a presentation short enough to leave demo time. Team size for winners ranged 2–6, most commonly 2–4.

### Cited Findings
- Team sizes of named winners: Plattr 2; Kisan Mitra 2; CardioSense 2; Moneyमित्र 4; Padhai Check 4; Rayquaza EX 4; DreamOps 4; PragyanAI 4; PhonePe internal teams 5–6 — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/agupta28/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/06/crop-insurance-conversational-analytics-ai-for-bharat-hackathon); [CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/mumbaihacks-2025-crowns-indias-next-gen-ai-innovators-becomes-worlds-largest-agentic-ai-hackathon/); [IIIT-Delhi](https://www.iiitd.ac.in/sites/default/files/docs/pressreleases/2026/AWS%20Hackathon%20press%20note_iiitd-April%202026.pdf); [Lightspeed](https://x.com/LightspeedIndia/status/1937084673496404199); [PhonePe](https://www.phonepe.com/blog/life-at-phonepe/1000-phonepe-rs-110-hacks-24-hours-of-innovation/)

- Live in-room demo is the format at Paytm (18:00–20:00 demo block), Sarvam BuildIn' ("one chance to demo to an expert jury"), MumbaiHacks (overnight build then judging), Google Agentic AI Day (30-hour offline); video pitch is the format at Razorpay Buildathon/Day0 and the Hack2skill idea stage — [Luma](https://luma.com/evm2zg68); [Zo House](https://blrxzo.substack.com/p/1-in-62-made-it-in-sarvam-ais-hackathon); [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/buildathon/); [Hack2skill](https://hack2skill.com/event/ai-for-bharat)

### Inferences
- Pattern 1 (confirmed-ish): working prototype in the room beats polish of slides; two SIH accounts and the Sarvam judge all say this. Pattern 2 (inference): with Sarvam as partner at Paytm, Indic voice is expected of everyone, so the differentiator is the action loop and guardrails, not the ASR. Pattern 3 (inference): compliance shows up as a tiebreaker in fintech juries (BoB NIDAAN "practical application in secure digital banking"; FinShield practicality), so showing deterministic limits around money movement is likely to score. Pattern 4 (inference): business-model clarity is rarely a scored line in India; "impact for millions of Indians" language is what organisers use instead, so frame scale not revenue. Pattern 5 (inference): a 2–3 person team is normal for winners and matches Paytm's cap. Pattern 6 (inference): make the demo survive bad Wi-Fi (local seed data, cached audio) because connectivity failures are the most-cited demo killer.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data exists on how many finalists at any in-scope event used voice vs screen, or merchant vs consumer; all pattern claims rest on 10–15 named winners.
- Zero published Paytm winner lists or judge commentary means Paytm-specific preferences are entirely inferred from track names, partner stack, and schedule.
