# Market, Evidence and Regulatory Frame for a Vernacular Voice "CFO Agent" for Indian Small Merchants (as of 31 Oct 2026)

Scope note: numbers below are from web searches on 31 Oct 2026. Primary sources (NPCI data via mirrors, RBI Directions text, Meta/Sarvam pricing pages, TransUnion CIBIL/SIDBI) were fetched where possible. Where only vendor blogs or secondary aggregators were available this is flagged. "(estimate)" marks any number that is a modelled or vendor-claimed figure rather than an official count.

## Key Question 1: Market numbers (merchants, UPI, udhaar, MSME credit gap, devices, language)

### Takeaway
Roughly 13 million kirana stores and 65–70 million digitally-enabled merchants sit on top of ~15 billion UPI P2M transactions a month (Sept 2026); 94% of surveyed small merchants accept UPI, 71% run their business on a smartphone, and only 41% of registered MSMEs have ever accessed formal credit against a SIDBI-estimated ₹30 lakh crore addressable credit gap. Hard, recent, independent data on udhaar (shop credit) per store does not exist; only vendor estimates are available.

### Cited Findings

**Merchant universe**
- India has an estimated 13 million kirana stores (FICCI figure; Cornell cites ~13 million in 2021 contributing ~11% of GDP and 8% of the workforce) — [Cornell Business Centers, May 2026](https://business.cornell.edu/centers/2026/05/13/indias-digital-pull-revolution/); [Chitrangana citing FICCI/Invest India](https://www.chitrangana.com/india-retail-transformation-2030/)
- Invest India expects only ~1.4 million of ~13 million kiranas to complete a formal modernisation (estimate) — [Chitrangana](https://www.chitrangana.com/india-retail-transformation-2030/)
- NPCI–BCG report ("India's Digital Payments Revolution: UPI's Global Impact", 2025) estimated soundboxes and interoperable QR had brought 65–70 million merchants onto digital payments, mostly small roadside vendors, tea stalls and kiranas (estimate) — [Business Standard, Aug 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/small-merchants-built-upi-s-reach-here-s-what-government-data-shows-126081700057_1.html)
- India has around 4.9 million street vendors — [SAGE Journal of Asian and African Studies, 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00219096231218437)
- Paytm Q2 FY27 (quarter ended 30 Sept 2026, released 26 Oct 2026): 5.2 crore registered merchants (+10% YoY); 1.64 crore merchants on subscription (device) plans, +27 lakh over the year; merchant GMV ₹7.1 lakh crore (+26%) — [Paytm investor blog](https://paytm.com/blog/investor-relations/paytm-q2-fy27-results-pat-225-crore-ebitda-rises-90-to-270-crore/); [INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/stocks/paytm-q2-results-profit-growth-analysis)

**UPI acceptance and volumes**
- A government-commissioned study (released Feb 2026, done in consultation with NPCI; 10,378 respondents across 15 states incl. 2,199 merchants) found 94% of small merchants surveyed had adopted UPI — [Business Standard, Aug 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/small-merchants-built-upi-s-reach-here-s-what-government-data-shows-126081700057_1.html)
- Kirana Club survey (Feb 2024, platform of ~18 lakh kirana owners, 10 states): 69% use Paytm QR for daily store transactions — [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/finance/42-indian-kiranas-have-started-to-use-other-payment-apps/469566)
- UPI September 2026: 24.07 billion transactions worth ₹29.37 lakh crore; 802 million/day; P2M = 15.22 billion transactions (~63% of volume), P2P = 8.83 billion; average ticket ₹1,012 — [Medianama, Oct 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-upi-september-transactions-decline/); [Dataful mirror of NPCI P2P/P2M series](https://dataful.in/datasets/431/)
- September 2026 P2M value ≈ ₹8.87 lakh crore (886,987 crore); 96% of P2M transactions were ≤ ₹2,000 by count, but transactions above ₹2,000 were 67% of P2M value — [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-upi-september-transactions-decline/); [Dataful](https://dataful.in/datasets/431/)
- H1 FY27 (Apr–Sep 2026): ~145 billion UPI transactions worth ₹177 lakh crore, +27% YoY by volume — [DD India, Oct 2026](https://ddindia.co.in/2026/10/upi-transactions-surge-23-to-24-07-billion-in-september-npci/)
- UPI remains free for consumers and small merchants (PCI clarification after the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill 2026 created an enabling frame for MDR); no binding UPI MDR notified as of the sources — [Lawbeat](https://lawbeat.in/news-updates/upi-will-stay-free-for-consumers-and-small-merchants-payments-council-of-india-clarifies-1620373); [Innoviti](https://innoviti.com/blogs/upi-mdr-2026-merchant-guide/)

**Udhaar (informal shop credit) — all estimates, vendor-sourced**
- Accountune claims a survey of 12,000+ kirana owners (Jan–Apr 2026) found ₹8,200 average monthly udhaar never recovered per store (estimate; vendor survey, methodology not published) — [Accountune](https://accountune.com/kirana-store-billing-software)
- ERP vendor claims typical kirana extends credit to 50–100 families and total outstanding udhaar can run ₹2–5 lakh (estimate) — [ERP Group](https://www.erpgroup.in/blog/billing-software-for-kirana-store-india); SuperK franchise guide gives a far lower ₹10,000–50,000 outstanding (estimate) — [SuperK](https://www.superk.in/blogs/franchise-hub/kirana-store-cost-india-2026). The two conflict by an order of magnitude.
- BizKhata claims 60–70% of kirana transactions involve some form of customer credit (estimate, vendor) — [BizKhata](https://bizkhata.in/templates/kirana-grocery-shop-billing-software/)
- Kiranas serve 230 million+ low/middle-income households buying in ₹100–200 lots and depending on credit between wage payments — [NRFHH journal article](https://www.nrfhh.com/index.php/journal/article/download/3314/1930/7435)
- Khatabook: 5 crore+ downloads, targets the 6.3-crore MSME base; no published per-merchant udhaar statistics found — [SaaSworthy comparison](https://www.saasworthy.com/compare/khatabook-vs-okcredit?pIds=8396,31224)

**MSME credit gap and formal credit access**
- TransUnion CIBIL–SIDBI MSME Pulse, July 2026: 8.7 crore registered MSMEs (Dec 2025); only 3.6 crore (41%) have ever accessed formal credit (Mar 2026); new-to-credit share of originations fell from 52% (FY23) to 42% (FY26); outstanding commercial credit ₹65.8 lakh crore across 4.4 crore active trades; delinquency stable at 1.8%; individual-borrower-led business lending grew 1.8x (Mar 2023–Mar 2026) vs 1.5x for entities — [TransUnion CIBIL MSME Pulse July 2026](https://www.transunioncibil.com/lp/msme-pulse-jul-2026); [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/nation/just-41-msmes-have-accessed-formal-credit-finds-report-1968144)
- SIDBI "Understanding Indian MSME Sector" (May 2025): total finance demand ₹123 lakh crore; debt demand ₹92 lakh crore; addressable debt demand ₹64 lakh crore; formal supply ₹34 lakh crore; addressable gap ₹30 lakh crore (~24% of debt demand). 17% of surveyed MSMEs have never availed credit; 8% rely on informal sources — [SIDBI PDF](https://www.sidbi.in/uploads/Understanding_Indian_MSME_sector_Progress_and_Challenges_13_05_25_Final.pdf); [BW Businessworld](https://www.businessworld.in/article/sidbi-pegs-msme-credit-gap-at-rs-30-lakh-cr-24-of-demand-556647)
- World Bank Group (Oct 2026): India's MSMEs face an estimated $333 billion financing gap; ~$9.6 billion private financing mobilised since 2022 — [World Bank feature, 11 Oct 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2026/10/11/from-credit-gaps-to-growth-how-india-s-small-and-medium-firms-benefit-from-private-investment)
- IFC 2018 study (2017 reference year) put the MSME debt gap at ₹25.8 trillion — [GapsIndia summary](https://feeds.gapsindia.com/blog/msme-financing-gap); original 2012 IFC–Intellecap report: total demand ₹32.5 trillion, viable addressable debt gap ₹3.57 trillion ($71.4bn) — [IFC PDF](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/mgrt/msme-report-03-01-2013.pdf)
- Deloitte: credit gap ~₹25 lakh crore (Mar 2025); only 14% of MSMEs have formal credit access by its measure — [Irish Sun / Deloitte report coverage](https://www.irishsun.com/news/279151669/only-14-of-india-msmes-have-access-to-formal-credit-despite-digital-finance-boom-deloitte-report). Note the 14% (Deloitte) vs 41% (CIBIL) conflict is definitional (all MSMEs vs Udyam-registered).

**Devices and language**
- PayNearby MSME Digital Index 2025 (June 2025; 10,000 respondents incl. kiranas, recharge shops, medical shops, CSPs): 71% cite the smartphone as their primary business device (84% among women); UPI most-preferred mode at 48%, AePS 39%; 7% exploring AI/automation tools; 73% report higher income/efficiency from digital tools — [CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/73-msmes-report-business-growth-via-digital-adoption-led-by-upi-and-smartphones-paynearby-survey/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/07/nearly-half-of-msmes-polled-prefer-upi-for-busines-paynearby)
- Street vendor study, Sircilla (Telangana), 2023, n≈150: 84% owned a smartphone; lack of smartphone was the stated reason for non-adoption of digital payments — [TIJER 2023](https://tijer.org/tijer/papers/TIJER2307011.pdf)
- KPMG–Google "Indian Languages: Defining India's Internet" (Apr 2017; n=4,612 urban + 2,448 rural): 68% of users consider local-language digital content more reliable than English; 88% of Indian-language users more likely to respond to a vernacular ad; 60% cite limited language support as the biggest barrier to online services; 9 of 10 new internet users are Indian-language users — [Quartz](https://qz.com/india/972844/indias-internet-users-have-more-faith-in-content-thats-not-in-english-study-says); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/internet-users-report-google/); [Social Beat](https://www.socialbeat.in/blog/kpmg-google-study-on-regional-content/)

### Inferences
- A defensible TAM framing: 13M kiranas / 65–70M digital merchants / 1.64 crore Paytm device merchants, with ~15B P2M transactions a month giving a dense, daily cashflow signal that a "CFO agent" can read without the merchant typing anything.
- The CIBIL 41%-ever-accessed-credit and falling new-to-credit share (52% → 42%) support the pitch that formal credit penetration among small merchants is stalling, not just lagging.
- Udhaar is the weakest data point. Say "vendor surveys put unrecovered udhaar at roughly ₹8,000 a month per store and outstanding at ₹10k–₹5 lakh depending on the source" and label it an estimate; do not present a single hard number.
- Roughly 3 in 10 small merchants do not run their business on a smartphone (PayNearby), which is the quantitative case for a voice/no-screen channel.

### Gaps
- No official (RBI/NSSO/NABARD) statistic on udhaar outstanding per kirana or share of kirana sales on credit; only vendor marketing numbers exist.
- No national smartphone-vs-feature-phone split specifically for merchants; PayNearby's 71% "primary device" is the best proxy.
- No post-2017 large-sample vernacular-preference study surfaced; the KPMG–Google 2017 figures are dated.
- NPCI's own monthly P2M statistics page was not fetched directly; figures come from Medianama/Dataful mirrors that match NPCI.

## Key Question 2: Evidence that voice / vernacular / no-screen outreach works for merchants

### Takeaway
Soundbox is the strongest proof that a no-screen, audio-first product scales (1.64 crore Paytm subscriptions, "deployed every 6 seconds"), and multilingual IVR has run at scale for a decade (Gram Vaani); but rigorous evidence on voice-bot collections or payment-reminder effectiveness in India is thin and almost entirely vendor-reported. The best-identified RCT evidence on reminders is from the Philippines and Uganda, not India.

### Cited Findings

**Soundbox / no-screen adoption**
- Paytm: 1.64 crore merchants on subscription with Soundbox "deployed at 1.64 crore storefronts" (Q2 FY27); Paytm positions the device as a "merchant operating system" and gateway for loan distribution — [Paytm Q2 FY27](https://paytm.com/blog/investor-relations/paytm-q2-fy27-results-pat-225-crore-ebitda-rises-90-to-270-crore/)
- Trajectory: 13.7 million device subscriptions (Sept 2025) → 1.5 crore (Q4 FY26) → 1.64 crore (Q2 FY27); Paytm has integrated AI into soundboxes for business insights, customer notifications and merchant support, and plans to monetise via subscription and "inference fees" — [YourStory, Nov 2025](https://yourstory.com/2025/11/paytms-q2-ai-soundbox-cautious-postpaid-revival-bnpl-lending); [Inc42, May 2026](https://inc42.com/buzz/npci-to-roll-out-unified-soundbox-infrastructure-for-merchants-report/)
- Paytm claims a Soundbox is deployed every 6 seconds — [Paytm blog](https://paytm.com/blog/investor-relations/paytm-soundbox-deployed-every-6-seconds/)
- NPCI is reportedly building a unified/interoperable UPI soundbox infrastructure (May 2026) — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/npci-to-roll-out-unified-soundbox-infrastructure-for-merchants-report/)
- Paytm attributes part of its indirect-cost reduction (52% → 46% of revenue) to AI in customer support, merchant servicing, fraud and collections — [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/stocks/paytm-q2-fy27-results-net-profit-rises-as-revenue-and-ebitda-grow/)

**Voice/IVR platforms at scale in rural India**
- Gram Vaani's Mobile Vaani: missed-call IVR, no data or smartphone needed; 100,000+ unique monthly active users and ~10,000 calls/day across Bihar, Jharkhand, MP (2019); volunteer network in 1,000+ panchayats assisting with digital financial services — [arXiv 1912.03457](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.03457); [The Hard Copy](https://thehardcopy.co/reaching-rural-communities-with-gram-vaani/)
- Gram Vaani 2025 paper: push vs pull IVR — usage rose only with repeated training and group solidarity; without training, usage in new geographies stayed low, prompting a need to collect phone numbers at scale to push messages — [arXiv 2504.18948](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18948)
- Mobile Vaani health pilot (Nalanda, Bihar): only 23% of women interviewed had heard of the platform — a caution on organic reach — [BMJ Open via PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9558784/)

**Voice bots / IVR in collections (vendor-reported)**
- Exotel pilot framework worked example (illustrative, not a disclosed client): connect rate 34%, right-party-contact 23%, PTP 13% of RPC, kept-PTP 55%, net recovery 9.4% vs 8% baseline, ROI 1.66x (estimate) — [Exotel](https://exotel.com/blog/ai-voicebot-roi-collections/)
- Exotel industry claims: 34–36% collection-cost reduction, 10–25% recovery improvement, 48% of NBFCs using AI voice/chat for collections (secondary-sourced vendor claims; estimate) — [Exotel](https://exotel.com/blog/ai-voice-agents-india-debt-collection-exotel/)
- DPDzero (voice agent "Tara"): co-founder says results are "a mixed bag"; the agent sometimes cannot comprehend dialects, but in tier-1 and some tier-2 markets performs as well as a human; company claims 40–60% lower cost per resolved case (estimate) — [Analytics India Magazine](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-origins-evolution/india-general-elections-twitter-bots-hashtags); [DPDzero blog](https://www.dpdzero.com/blogs/voice-ai-debt-collection-india)
- SquadStack: legacy IVR cannot extract a promise-to-pay, only play a reminder; Kissht deployment ran Hinglish as primary language; pilots are gated on PTP rate or roll-back % — [SquadStack](https://www.squadstack.ai/blog/ai-voice-agents-for-loan-collections-in-india-pre-due-to-recovery)

**Reminder effectiveness (academic, non-India)**
- Karlan, Morten & Zinman (NBER w17952, Philippines, 1,703 loans): average SMS reminder did not significantly improve repayment; only messages naming the loan officer robustly improved repayment — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17952/w17952.pdf)
- Cadena & Schoar (NBER w17020, Uganda): SMS reminders had an impact on repayment comparable to monetary incentives — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17020/w17020.pdf)
- India RCTs found were on repayment flexibility (Field, Pande et al., Kolkata), not reminders — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3458929/)

**UPI collect requests**
- NPCI banned all P2P collect requests from 1 Oct 2025 (no P2P collect "initiated, routed or processed" after 2 Oct 2025) to curb fraud; earlier capped at ₹2,000 — [Business Standard, Aug 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/upi-collect-requests-to-end-from-october-here-s-how-it-changes-payments-125081500767_1.html)
- Verified-merchant (P2M) collect requests continue (e.g. Amazon, Swiggy, IRCTC), but a March 2025 report said NPCI was considering phasing out merchant collect too in favour of push/QR — [Business Standard, Mar 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/upi-collect-call-transactions-phase-out-npci-fraud-125031800575_1.html)
- P2M daily limit raised to ₹10 lakh for selected verified categories from 15 Sept 2025; P2P stays ₹1 lakh — [NewsOnAir](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/npci-hikes-upi-limit-for-merchant-payments-to-%E2%82%B910-lakh-per-day)

### Inferences
- Soundbox is the cleanest analogy: merchants already pay ~₹100/month (historical) for an audio-only device, so "voice-first" has a paid precedent, and Paytm itself has said it will charge "inference fees" on AI soundboxes.
- The Karlan et al. finding (personalisation drives reminder effectiveness) is a usable design principle: a named, familiar agent voice should outperform generic robocalls.
- A kirana sending udhaar reminders to customers via UPI collect is now impossible for unverified/P2P flows; a merchant-side agent must use the merchant's verified P2M collect or push a QR/payment link instead.
- Vendor collections numbers can be quoted only as "industry claims"; a judge will discount them.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or RCT evidence on voice-bot or IVR collection outcomes in India.
- No published data on UPI collect-request acceptance rates or payment-reminder effectiveness from NPCI or any PSP.
- No study found of voice-based financial services specifically for merchants (CGAP, Dvara, IDinsight searches did not surface merchant-focused voice pilots in the time available).

## Key Question 3: Regulation for outbound AI calls, loan offers, AI in finance, data protection

### Takeaway
Four regimes stack on an outbound vernacular loan-offer call: TRAI's TCCCPR (as amended Feb 2025: 140-series for promotional, 1600-series for transactional/service, preference scrubbing, 7-day consent validity, 5-complaint threshold), RBI's Digital Lending Directions 2025 (KFS, ≥1-day cooling-off, direct disbursal/repayment with no LSP pass-through, no auto credit-limit increase, 5% DLG cap, explicit purpose-wise consent, no contact-list access), the advisory FREE-AI framework (disclose AI, explainability, board AI policy), and the DPDP Act with Rules notified 13 Nov 2025 (consent managers live 13 Nov 2026, full obligations 13 May 2027; voice recordings are personal data).

### Cited Findings

**TRAI TCCCPR 2018 and 2025 amendment**
- TRAI issued the TCCCPR (Second Amendment) Regulations on 12 Feb 2025; most provisions effective 30 days later — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e3b6f601-67ae-40e3-9a05-6bd3472925a4); [Sigma Chambers](https://www.sigmachambers.in/post/2025-tcccpr-amendments-a-renewed-push-by-trai-for-order-in-commercial-communications-1)
- Promotional calls must use the 140 series; transactional and service calls the new 1600 series — [FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/446114/trai-tcccpr-2025-tougher-rules-spam-calls-sms.html)
- Promotional communication may only go to subscribers who have not blocked the category in the Preference Register or who have given consent in the Consent Register; explicit consent overrides a full block — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/trai-s-crackdown-on-spam-calls-and-ai-driven-telemarketing)
- Consent for ongoing transactions valid only 7 days; inferred consent for service/transactional messaging valid only for contract duration; senders may not re-seek consent for 90 days after an opt-out — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e3b6f601-67ae-40e3-9a05-6bd3472925a4)
- Mixed content (promotional + service/transactional) is treated as promotional — [Sigma Chambers](https://www.sigmachambers.in/post/2025-tcccpr-amendments-a-renewed-push-by-trai-for-order-in-commercial-communications-1)
- Complaint threshold cut from 10 to 5; TSP action timeline cut from 30 to 5 days; AI-based spam detection mandated for operators; complaints against unregistered senders allowed without prior preference registration — [Rootle](https://rootle.ai/blog/the-february-2025-trai-amendment-voice-ai-compliance/)
- Calling hours: sources conflict. The 2018 regulation's default blocked band is 9 PM–9 AM (calls allowed 9 AM–9 PM, user-selectable bands); one compliance guide claims 10 AM–7 PM — [FreJun](https://frejun.com/trai-compliance-call-centers/); [Botsense](https://botsense.io/blog/trai-rules-outbound-calling-india/). The official text was not readable (TRAI March 2026 draft PDF fetched as binary). Treat 9 AM–9 PM as the TRAI default and 8 AM–7 PM as the stricter RBI recovery window (below).
- June 2025: TRAI–RBI pilot on Digital Consent Acquisition requiring explicit consent for telemarketing — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/trai-s-crackdown-on-spam-calls-and-ai-driven-telemarketing)
- March 2026 TRAI draft consultation proposes treating consent-based "service" communication as promotional (removing the differentiation) — [TRAI draft CP, 13 Mar 2026 (PDF)](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-03/Draft_CP_13032026_0.pdf) (summary via search; text not extractable)

**RBI Digital Lending Directions 2025 (RBI/2025-26/36, 8 May 2025; replaces Sept 2022 Guidelines, June 2023 DLG circular, June 2020 FPC/outsourcing circular)**
- Para 8(i): RE shall provide a Key Fact Statement; APR defined per RBI's KFS circular (Para 4(i)) — [RBI Master Direction](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12848)
- Para 10(i)–(ii): cooling-off period set by RE board, "not being less than one day"; borrower may exit paying principal plus proportionate APR "without any penalty"; a reasonable one-time processing fee allowed if disclosed in KFS (minimum reduced from 3 days in 2022 to 1 day) — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12848); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/finance-and-banking/rbi-notifies-reserve-bank-of-india-digital-lending-directions-2025)
- Para 9(i)–(v): disbursal only into the borrower's bank account; repayment directly to the RE's account "without any pass-through account/pool account of any third party, including the accounts of LSP"; LSPs may not control fund flows; LSP fees paid by RE, never collected from borrower — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12848)
- Para 7(ii): "no automatic increase in credit limit unless an explicit request is received, evaluated and kept on record" — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12848)
- Para 12(i)–(iv): data collection need-based with "prior and explicit consent of the borrower having audit trail"; DLAs must not access contact lists/call logs; one-time camera/mic/location access only for KYC with explicit consent; borrower can deny/revoke consent per data item; purpose disclosed at each stage; explicit consent before sharing with third parties — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12848)
- Para 8(v): recovery agent particulars sent by email/SMS before the agent contacts the borrower; Para 11: nodal grievance officers at RE and LSP, displayed on website, DLA and KFS; unresolved 30-day complaints escalate to RBI CMS — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12848)
- Para 23(i): DLG cover "shall not exceed five per cent" of the disbursed portfolio; forms: cash, lien-marked FD, bank guarantee; invoke within 120 days; only with LSPs incorporated under Companies Act 2013 — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12848); [ELP](https://elplaw.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Regulatory-Update-on-RBIs-Consolidated-Digital-Lending-Framework-%E2%80%93-May-2025.pdf)
- RE remains fully liable for LSP acts/omissions; DLAs to be reported on RBI CIMS by 15 June 2025; multi-lender LSP rules (Para 6, each offer shows RE name, amount, tenor, APR, repayment, KFS link) effective 1 Nov 2025 — [Argus Partners](https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/rbi-digital-lending-directions-2025-an-overview/)
- RBI's stated concerns motivating the Directions: "unbridled engagement of third parties, mis-selling, breach of data privacy, unfair business conduct, charging of exorbitant interest rates, and unethical recovery practices" — [RBI circular index](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=12848)
- Feb 2026: IRACP Amendment Directions 2026 reinstated DLG recognition in NBFC loss estimates — [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/rbi-eases-default-loss-guarantee-rules-for-nbfcs-in-digital-lending-framework)
- Recovery contact window 8 AM–7 PM under RBI's Aug 2022 recovery-agent circular / Fair Practices Code, applying to calls, SMS, WhatsApp (secondary sources only) — [CredSettle](https://www.credsettle.com/rbi-guidelines-calling-after-7pm); [Freed](https://freed.care/blog/rbi-guidelines-recovery-agents)

**RBI FREE-AI (13 Aug 2025) and follow-ons**
- Committee report (chair Pushpak Bhattacharyya, IIT Bombay), 7 Sutras (Trust, People First, Innovation, Fairness, Accountability, Explainability, Resilience), 6 pillars, 26 recommendations; advisory, not binding — [RBI PDF](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2025/09/rbis-free-ai-committee-report-in-the-financial-sector.html)
- Recommendations include board-approved AI policy, AI in audits, lifecycle governance, "making consumers aware when they are dealing with AI", AI incident reporting, AI disclosures in annual reports, graded liability — [Khaitan & Co](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2025-08/Ergo%20-%20FREE%20AI%20Framework%20-%2028%20Augusut%202025.pdf)
- The report itself flags hallucination, bias and lack of explainability as risks and notes institutions have been cautious about customer-facing use cases — [RBI PDF](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF)
- RBI draft Guidance on Regulatory Principles for Model Risk Management issued 24 June 2026 (draft) — [BankingNewsAI](https://www.bankingnewsai.com/ai-regulation/rbi); [CyberSigma](https://cybersigmacs.com/blog/rbi-ai-guidelines-model-risk-management-2026/)
- Vendor LLM use does not shift responsibility; RE remains accountable for governance and validation — [Scrut](https://www.scrut.io/post/rbi-framework-for-responsible-and-ethical-enablement-of-artificial-intelligence)

**Mis-selling and ombudsman**
- RBI Ombudsman offices received ~2.96 lakh complaints in 2024-25; loans/advances, cards, mobile banking and deposits were ~four-fifths — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/amp/business/rbi-to-issue-norms-to-curb-mis-selling-of-financial-products-1927059)
- Draft RBI mis-selling rules: full refund plus compensation on proven mis-selling; Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2026 effective 1 July 2026 — [TradingView/Moneycontrol](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moneycontrol:3884f15b9094b:0-fighting-dark-patterns-what-new-rbi-draft-mis-selling-rules-mean-for-banks-customers/); [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/how-to-claim-a-refund-for-bank-mis-selling-under-the-new-rbi-framework)

**DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025**
- DPDP Rules notified 13 Nov 2025 (gazette 14 Nov per Wikipedia), 18-month phased rollout: Data Protection Board provisions immediate; Rule 4 Consent Managers from 13 Nov 2026 (Indian company, ₹2 crore net worth); full substantive compliance (notice, consent, security, breach reporting, data-principal rights) 13 May 2027 — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/dpdp-act-and-rules-2025-the-2026-compliance-milestones-businesses-cant-afford-to-miss); [MirvoLegal, Sept 2026](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Rules,_2025)
- Proposal to shorten the timeline to Nov 2026 reported but not notified as of Sept 2026 — [MirvoLegal](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/)
- Voice recordings and transcripts are personal data; voiceprints are biometric; consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, purpose-bound and as easy to withdraw as to give; sign-up consent does not extend to new voice use cases (collections, cross-sell, model training are distinct purposes) — [Caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/dpdp-act-compliance-checklist-voice-ai-india); [Rootle](https://rootle.ai/blog/dpdp-act-voice-ai-consent-compliant-calling/) (vendor sources)
- Penalties up to ₹250 crore per violation; ₹50 crore for processing without valid consent (one vendor claims ₹500 crore; conflicting) — [Caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/voice-ai-compliance-india-2026)
- Cross-border STT/TTS processing is a cross-border transfer under DPDP; RBI data-localisation already requires Indian storage for banks/NBFCs — [Fonix](https://fonix.ai/dpdp-compliance)

**NPCI**
- P2P collect banned from 1 Oct 2025; verified P2M collect continues; P2M daily limit ₹10 lakh for selected categories from 15 Sept 2025 (see KQ2) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/upi-collect-requests-to-end-from-october-here-s-how-it-changes-payments-125081500767_1.html); [NewsOnAir](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/npci-hikes-upi-limit-for-merchant-payments-to-%E2%82%B910-lakh-per-day)

### Inferences
- A loan offer delivered by a proactive AI call is "promotional" under TCCCPR unless the merchant has given explicit, registered consent; it must go out on a 140-series number, respect preference scrubbing, and the consent is time-bounded. The safest design is opt-in (merchant asks the Saathi, or consents at onboarding via the Paytm app/Soundbox) rather than cold outbound.
- The agent can present offers only as an LSP on behalf of an RE; it cannot touch funds (Para 9), cannot auto-raise limits (Para 7(ii)), must surface the KFS and APR before acceptance, and must leave ≥1-day cooling-off. "Agent closes the loan on the call" is non-compliant; "agent explains the KFS and the merchant confirms in-app" is defensible.
- Disclosing that the caller is an AI is a FREE-AI recommendation and a DPDP/TRAI best practice; the pitch should show it in the first sentence of every call.
- Recording and transcribing merchant calls requires its own consent purpose; using recordings to train models requires a separate consent.
- The 8 AM–7 PM RBI recovery window is narrower than TRAI's default 9 AM–9 PM; use 9 AM–7 PM as the intersection for anything touching credit.

### Gaps
- Official TRAI text for 2025 calling-hour bands could not be read (PDF binary); verify against TCCCPR 2018 Schedule before quoting a window.
- RBI's 8 AM–7 PM recovery window is sourced only from secondary blogs; the primary circular (RBI/2022-23/108, 12 Aug 2022) was not fetched.
- No specific NPCI circular on "payment reminders" or on merchant-initiated collect frequency limits was found.
- No RBI rule found that specifically addresses AI-generated financial statements or voice agents making credit offers; FREE-AI is advisory and the MRM guidance is draft.

## Key Question 5: Risks and failure modes a fintech judge will raise

### Takeaway
Judges will challenge (1) spam fatigue and TRAI exposure of proactive calls, (2) mis-selling under RBI's tightening regime, (3) hallucinated numbers in a "CFO" statement with no explainability, (4) STT failure on dialects and noisy shopfronts (district-level WER swings of 4%–44% for the same model), and (5) DPDP consent and cross-border voice data. Each has a documented regulatory or empirical hook.

### Cited Findings

**Spam fatigue / trust**
- TRAI cut the spam-complaint threshold to 5 complaints and imposed 5-day TSP action; AI-based spam detection is mandatory for operators — [Rootle](https://rootle.ai/blog/the-february-2025-trai-amendment-voice-ai-compliance/)
- Mixed promotional + service content is reclassified promotional; a service call that slips in a loan offer becomes a promotional call needing consent — [Sigma Chambers](https://www.sigmachambers.in/post/2025-tcccpr-amendments-a-renewed-push-by-trai-for-order-in-commercial-communications-1)
- Karlan et al.: generic reminders had no significant repayment effect; personalisation did — [NBER w17952](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17952/w17952.pdf)
- Gram Vaani: push IVR usage stayed low without repeated training; only 23% awareness in a Bihar pilot — [arXiv 2504.18948](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18948); [PMC9558784](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9558784/)
- Street-vendor studies cite trust deficits, fraud fear, digital illiteracy and connectivity as barriers — [IJSREM 2025](https://ijsrem.com/download/digital-payment-adoption-and-its-challenges-among-kirana-stores-in-the-post-pandemic-era); [Zenodo 2026](https://zenodo.org/records/23435847)

**Mis-selling of loans**
- RBI's Digital Lending Directions explicitly cite "mis-selling" as a motivating concern; draft mis-selling rules require full refund plus compensation; Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2026 from 1 July 2026; ~2.96 lakh ombudsman complaints in 2024-25 — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=12848); [TradingView/Moneycontrol](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moneycontrol:3884f15b9094b:0-fighting-dark-patterns-what-new-rbi-draft-mis-selling-rules-mean-for-banks-customers/); [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/amp/business/rbi-to-issue-norms-to-curb-mis-selling-of-financial-products-1927059)
- Para 7(ii) bars automatic credit-limit increases; Para 10 mandates ≥1-day cooling-off; Para 6 requires APR-comparable offer display on multi-lender platforms — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12848)

**Hallucination / explainability**
- RBI FREE-AI report names hallucination, bias and lack of explainability as technology risks and records institutional caution on customer-facing use; "Explainability" is one of 7 Sutras; recommends consumers be told when dealing with AI — [RBI FREE-AI PDF](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF)
- Compliance commentary: RBI expects model/system-level hallucination controls, not disclaimers; draft Model Risk Management guidance (24 June 2026) layers on top — [CyberSigma](https://cybersigmacs.com/blog/rbi-ai-guidelines-model-risk-management-2026/)
- SSRN critique: FREE-AI lacks means to translate principles into controls — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6925198)

**Indic STT accuracy in noise and dialect**
- "Voice of India" district-level benchmark: WER for the same ASR model ranges 4% to 44% across districts; Odia (best 35.1%), Bhojpuri (33.3%), Maithili, Chhattisgarhi worst — [Vividh-ASR / adalat-ai blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/adalat-ai/vividh-benchmark)
- Vividh-ASR (Hindi, Malayalam): existing Indic ASR is biased to studio/broadcast audio and degrades on spontaneous, noisy, non-professional-hardware speech; zero-shot Whisper WER routinely exceeds 100% for Malayalam and sometimes Hindi — [arXiv 2605.13087](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13087)
- Sarvam Saaras V3: ~19% WER on IndicVoices (19.31% on top-10 languages), improved from ~22% for V2.5 — [Sarvam blog](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/asr)
- Gnani Prisma v2.5 vendor claim: <4% WER, first in 8 of 9 languages on Kathbath Noisy 8kHz (vendor claim; narrowband benchmark not comparable to clean 16kHz) — [Gnani](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/blogs/how-gnanis-proprietary-asr-works-in-12-indian-languages-breaking-down-language-barriers-in-speech-recognition)
- Interspeech 2023 on noisy code-mixed MUCS/GramVaani/call-center data: baseline WER 30.69 improved to 16.95 — [ISCA](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2023/verma23_interspeech.pdf)
- Indic DiarBench: far-field conversational audio with reverberation and noise is a distinct, harder regime — [arXiv 2607.23808](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23808)
- DPDzero field experience: voice agent "sometimes unable to comprehend dialects" — [Analytics India Magazine](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-origins-evolution/india-general-elections-twitter-bots-hashtags)
- Gnani describes the "rural India ASR problem" (2G networks, low-end devices, background noise) — [Gnani research](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/research/the-rural-india-asr-problem-engineering-speech-recognition-for-background-noise)

**Data privacy**
- Voice = personal data; voiceprint = biometric; consent purpose-bound; penalties up to ₹250 crore; cross-border STT/TTS is a restricted transfer; RBI localisation applies to REs — [Caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/dpdp-act-compliance-checklist-voice-ai-india); [Fonix](https://fonix.ai/dpdp-compliance)
- Digital Lending Para 12: no contact-list/call-log access; explicit audit-trailed consent per purpose; consent revocable — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12848)

### Inferences
- The judge-proof framing is "merchant-initiated or merchant-consented, in-network (Paytm app/Soundbox) voice, with every number read back traceable to a UPI ledger line" rather than cold outbound calling with an LLM freely narrating finances.
- Hallucination defence must be architectural: the CFO's numbers come from deterministic aggregation of settlement data; the LLM only phrases them; show the audit trail. Quote FREE-AI's explainability Sutra and the June 2026 draft MRM guidance as the bar.
- STT defence: cite Saaras V3/V4 WER (~19% on IndicVoices) honestly, add confirmation loops ("Did you say ₹500 to Ramesh?"), constrain vocabulary (amounts, names from the ledger), and fall back to keypad/WhatsApp. Do not claim "works in all 22 languages" without benchmark caveats.
- Loan risk defence: the agent is an LSP surface only; it explains the KFS, never auto-accepts, never touches funds, enforces cooling-off, and logs consent (Paras 6–12, 23).

### Gaps
- No published empirical hallucination rate for financial LLM assistants in India.
- No benchmark specifically on STT accuracy in a shopfront/market-noise setting; Kathbath Noisy and Vividh Tier C/D are the closest proxies.
- No consumer-research data on Indian merchants' tolerance for AI calls or stated preference for AI vs human callers.
